# Product Thinking Resources

## Knowledge

### Primary course (Institute of Digital Government, Singapore)

- [Product Thinking overview (IDG)](https://www.idg.gov.sg/product-thinking/)
  Landing page for the 7-module course inside the "AI Build 301" pathway. Defines product thinking as "a discipline of asking better questions before reaching for solutions." Use for: the three principles (problems before solutions, outcomes over outputs, Policy-Ops-Tech integration) and the module map.
- [Module 1: Understanding the Problem](https://www.idg.gov.sg/guides-and-resources/productthinking1/)
  Why projects fail despite strong execution: solving the wrong problem. Grab/Amazon/Netflix as "start narrow, then expand" examples. Use for: motivation and the three principles.
- [Module 2: Start With The Whys (Five Whys)](https://www.idg.gov.sg/guides-and-resources/productthinking2/)
  Five Whys as the tool that "moves you from symptom to cause." Key nuance: problems rarely have one root cause; pick causes that are within your control and closely connected to the outcome. Use for: root cause analysis practice.
- [Module 3: Craft a Clear Problem Statement (4Cs)](https://www.idg.gov.sg/guides-and-resources/productthinking3/)
  The 4Cs: Clarity, Consequence, Cause, Confirmation. Worked example: citizens repeatedly filling forms across government services. Use for: problem statement drafting and critique.
- [Module 4: Metrics](https://www.idg.gov.sg/guides-and-resources/productthinking4/)
  SMART metrics, leading vs lagging indicators, Value-Cost Ratio (VCR) as "one input alongside other considerations." Use for: choosing outcome metrics and locating them on the indicator chain.
- [Module 5: Assumptions and Risks](https://www.idg.gov.sg/guides-and-resources/productthinking5/)
  Three risk types (market, technical, team); four de-risking stages (proof of concept, proof of value, scale, maturity). Health appointment A/B case: zero vs 24 bookings. Use for: staged delivery and cheapest-test thinking.
- [Module 6: A Good Customer Experience](https://www.idg.gov.sg/guides-and-resources/productthinking6/)
  Why CX is non-optional for mandatory public services. The 11-Star Framework (imagine an absurdly great experience, work backwards to the feasible). ParkingSG and RedeemSG cases. Use for: ambition-stretching and trade-off exercises.
- [Module 7: Key Takeaways](https://www.idg.gov.sg/guides-and-resources/productthinking7/)
  Three mindset shifts: Solutions → Problems, Outputs → Outcomes, Big Bang → Staged Delivery. Toolkit recap: 5 Whys, 4Cs, SMART + VCR, Staged Delivery, 11-Star. Use for: review and spaced-retrieval quizzes.

### Primary sources behind the frameworks the course borrows

- [Book: _Outcomes Over Output_ by Josh Seiden](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/45186993-outcomes-over-output)
  Defines an outcome as "a change in human behavior that drives business results." Short book. Use for: the outputs vs outcomes distinction and for turning outputs into outcome statements.
- [Podcast: Masters of Scale, "Do things that don't scale" with Brian Chesky (2017)](https://mastersofscale.com/episode/brian-chesky/)
  Origin of the 11-Star Experience exercise. Transcript available. Use for: the 11-Star framework in Module 6.
- [Book: _Toyota Production System: Beyond Large-Scale Production_ by Taiichi Ohno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_whys)
  Origin of Five Whys: "by repeating why five times, the nature of the problem as well as its solution becomes clear." The Wikipedia entry carries the classic machine-stoppage example and the known criticisms (stopping at symptoms, single-cause bias). Use for: Module 2 and the pitfalls of Five Whys.
