# Mission: Product Thinking

## Why
You influence what your team builds and want to stop shipping features that miss the real problem. Product thinking gives you the habit of asking "what problem, for whom, and what would change?" before anyone reaches for a solution.

## Success looks like
- When a feature request lands, you can tell in under a minute whether it is a solution in disguise, and ask the question that surfaces the problem behind it.
- You can rewrite an output ("ship X") as an outcome ("Y changes for Z") and propose one metric that would show it.
- You can run a Five Whys on a team problem and land on a cause that is within your control.
- You can draft a problem statement using the 4Cs that a colleague could critique without you in the room.
- You can name the cheapest test that would de-risk a proposed build before committing to it.

## Constraints
- 10 minutes per session. Every lesson must be finishable in one sitting of that length.
- No live project yet. Lessons use realistic case studies, biased toward small-team and public-sector settings like the source course.
- Solo course. No community suggestions unless asked.

## Out of scope
- Product management as a career track (roadmapping tools, stakeholder politics, prioritisation frameworks like RICE).
- UX research methods in depth. Customer experience is covered only as far as the source course goes.
- The "AI Build" and vibecoding modules that follow this course in the IDG pathway.
