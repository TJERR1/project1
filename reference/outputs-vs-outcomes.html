Outputs vs Outcomes

A one-page test for telling the thing you ship from the change it is supposed to cause.

Definitions

TermMeaningSource
Output A thing the team makes or delivers. A feature, form, report, migration, training session, launch. Countable. Fully under the team's control. IDG: "the features delivered"
Outcome A change in what a named group of people do, or no longer have to do, once the output exists. Observable in the world, not in the backlog. IDG: "the change achieved". Seiden: "a change in human behavior that drives business results"

The test

Could we deliver this perfectly and still have nothing change for the people it is for?

Yes: it is an output.
No, because the statement already says what changes for whom: it is an outcome.

Two supporting checks:

  • Who is in the sentence? Outputs rarely name a user. Outcomes almost always do.
  • What verb do they do? "Ship", "build", "publish", "train", "migrate", "launch" belong to the team. "Complete", "stop phoning", "reuse", "unblock themselves" belong to users.

Traps

Looks likeActuallyWhy
"100% of staff trained"OutputTraining was delivered. Says nothing about whether staff use what they learned.
"Launch successfully, on time, on budget"OutputDescribes the quality of the delivery, not any change for users.
"More active users of the dashboard"OutcomeUsing the dashboard is a behaviour change. Whether it is the right outcome is a separate question.
"Improve the parent experience"Neither yetPoints at an outcome but names no observable change. Push for the behaviour.

Rewriting an output as an outcome

Fill the blanks: [Who] [does / no longer has to do what], [observable how].

OutputOutcome
Ship the redesigned enrolment formParents complete enrolment in one sitting without phoning the office
Build a chatbot for parent enquiriesParents get an accurate answer to routine questions within an hour, at any time of day, without a staff member typing it
Produce a weekly attendance reportCentre managers contact at-risk students within a week of a second missed session

If you cannot find a credible outcome for an output, that is the signal. The request is a solution in search of a problem. Ask what prompted it.

Checklist for an outcome statement

  1. Names who changes.
  2. Says what they do differently, or no longer have to do.
  3. Could be observed without asking the team what it shipped.
  4. Could fail even if the output ships perfectly.
